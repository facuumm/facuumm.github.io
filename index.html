JUAN FACUNDO MORICI

Neuroscientist | Postdoctoral Researcher

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the Center for Neuroscience, Sorbonne Université in Paris, working in the Girardeau Lab. My research focuses on how memories are formed, how they are stabilized during sleep, and how they subsequently influence behavior.

I hold a PhD degree in Basic Medical Science from the University of Buenos Aires. I am trained in behavioral and cognitive neuroscience, with expertise in in vivo electrophysiology in freely-moving animals. I combine high-density neural recordings with data analysis and computational modeling to study how neural population dynamics in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex support memory processes.

I am convinced that understanding the brain requires an integrative approach. My research spans multiple techniques—from behavior and optogenetics to electrophysiology—to dissect the neural circuits underlying memory retrieval, interference resolution, and sleep-dependent consolidation.

Current research interests:

  • Memory formation and its stabilization during sleep
  • How memories influence subsequent behavior
  • Hippocampal-prefrontal circuits supporting episodic memory

Ongoing research:

  • Hippocampal-prefrontal cortex network dynamics during context-dependent memory retrieval. I am interested in how the hippocampus and the medial prefrontal cortex interact to retrieve the appropriate memory when the cues that guide retrieval are associated with multiple memories.
  • Dorsal and ventral hippocampal representations of emotional experiences. Together with Azul Silva, we are interested in how the dorsal and ventral hippocampus represent different emotional experiences. We address this question by analyzing two datasets that we recorded, which include neurons from both regions while animals explore a rewarded context and an aversive context.

Experimental approaches: I record neural activity in freely-moving rats during behavior—memory-guided tasks, sleep, and naturalistic exploration—using multi-site electrophysiology. I combine these neural recordings with behavioral analysis to understand how neural population dynamics support adaptive behavior and memory processes. Signal processing and machine learning techniques allow me to decode behavioral correlates of neural activity.
